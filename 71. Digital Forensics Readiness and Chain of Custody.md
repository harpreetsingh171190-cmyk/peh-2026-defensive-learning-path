# Module 71 — Digital Forensics Readiness and Chain of Custody

## Outcomes
- Preserve evidence integrity and provenance.
- Minimize collection and access to sensitive information.
- Produce notes another analyst can reproduce.

## First response
Follow the organization's incident plan and applicable law. If an incident is active, prioritize safety and containment decisions with the authorized incident lead. Avoid unnecessary interaction with the affected system.

## Evidence record fields
- Case identifier and authority/approval
- Evidence item identifier and source
- Collector, date/time, timezone, and collection method
- Tool name/version and relevant command or procedure
- Hash algorithm and hash value when appropriate
- Storage location, access controls, and transfer history
- Every handoff, with sender, recipient, timestamp, and purpose
- Limitations, uncertainty, and any actions that could alter evidence

## Lab: synthetic evidence manifest
Create a manifest for three fictional files. Use dummy filenames and generated sample content. Compute hashes locally if desired; never upload real evidence to an unknown service.

## Good practice
- Preserve originals where feasible and analyze verified working copies.
- Synchronize and record time sources.
- Restrict access and avoid collecting unrelated personal data.
- Document tool limitations and preserve audit trails.
- Follow retention, disclosure, and legal-hold requirements.

## Deliverable
A chain-of-custody form plus a short timeline that clearly separates observed facts from hypotheses.
