# 64. Secrets Management and Credential Lifecycle

## Why it matters
Passwords, API keys, signing keys, tokens and certificates can grant access well beyond the application where they were first used. Treat every secret as a managed asset.

## Lifecycle checklist
- **Create:** generate unique, high-entropy credentials using approved tooling.
- **Store:** use a platform secret manager or protected credential store; do not hard-code secrets in source, mobile apps, images or logs.
- **Grant:** scope each identity to the minimum permissions and resources required.
- **Use:** prefer short-lived credentials and workload identity over long-lived static keys.
- **Monitor:** alert on unusual access and review secret access logs.
- **Rotate/revoke:** define owners, expiry and emergency revocation procedures.
- **Dispose:** remove old credentials and verify that backups and deployment artifacts are handled appropriately.

## If a secret is exposed
1. Revoke or rotate it immediately; assume deletion from a commit does not erase history.
2. Check audit logs for use during the exposure window.
3. Remove the secret from active artifacts and follow the repository/provider's cleanup guidance.
4. Document impact and prevention without copying the secret into the incident ticket.
