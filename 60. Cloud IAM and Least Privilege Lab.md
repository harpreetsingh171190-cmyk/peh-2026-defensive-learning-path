# 60. Cloud IAM and Least Privilege Lab

## Learning goals
- Explain users, groups, roles, policies, service identities, and temporary credentials.
- Recognize wildcard permissions and long-lived keys as common risk patterns.
- Review access without collecting or exposing secrets.

## Review process
1. Identify the cloud account/project and written authorization.
2. Export policy metadata through the provider's official read-only tooling.
3. Find unused identities, broad wildcards, public trust relationships, and long-lived credentials.
4. Map each permission to a task and owner; propose a least-privilege alternative.
5. Test changes in a sandbox, validate workload health, and maintain a rollback plan.
6. Rotate credentials through approved secret-management workflows; never paste secrets into reports.

## Exercise
Use a fictional policy with permissions `read:reports`, `write:billing`, and `admin:*`. Decide which permissions a read-only reporting service needs. Explain how you would test and approve a narrower policy.

## Deliverable
A permission review table: identity, current access, required task, proposed access, owner, test evidence, and review date.

## Guardrails
Do not enumerate accounts or test permissions in a cloud tenant without authorization. Avoid real access keys in labs; use provider sandboxes or synthetic policy documents.
