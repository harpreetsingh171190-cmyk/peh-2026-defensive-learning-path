# 33. Detection, Reporting and Professional Workflow

## A repeatable assessment workflow
1. Authorization and scope.
2. Asset and attack-surface inventory.
3. Threat model and test plan.
4. Controlled testing with timestamps and low-impact settings.
5. Evidence collection and secret redaction.
6. Finding validation and false-positive review.
7. Risk-based remediation.
8. Retest, cleanup, and final report.

## Evidence quality
For every finding, keep a timestamp, target identifier, exact request or command, expected result, observed result, and sanitized evidence. Preserve original evidence securely; use redacted copies in reports. Do not collect more data than necessary.

## Detection practice
In a local lab, generate benign authentication failures, web errors, and container start/stop events. Review logs for:
- unusual authentication patterns,
- repeated authorization failures,
- unexpected process or network activity,
- new or modified services,
- suspicious access to secrets,
- unusual outbound connections.

Create a small timeline and explain which observations are facts and which are hypotheses. Do not deploy persistence, evade monitoring, or disable defenses on systems outside an explicitly authorized exercise.

## Report structure
- Executive summary
- Scope, dates, exclusions, and limitations
- Methodology and tools
- Prioritized findings
- Reproduction steps and sanitized evidence
- Business/technical impact
- Remediation guidance
- Retest results
- Appendix: asset inventory and test log

## Severity
Use the client's agreed scoring method. Explain exploitability, required access, impact, affected assets, and uncertainty. A scanner severity is not automatically the final risk rating.

## Portfolio
Publish only a sanitized lab report: no client names, real IPs, tokens, personal information, or unauthorized screenshots. Include a clear statement that the work was performed in a controlled lab.
